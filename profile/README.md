# Meso-WX Lab

### Mesoscale Weather, Convection, and Atmospheric Extremes

Welcome to **Meso-WX**, a research group studying the physics, evolution, and predictability of high-impact weather.

We are interested in how atmospheric processes interact across scales, from clouds and convective cells to organized storm systems and regional weather extremes. Our work combines **in situ and remotely-sensed observations, numerical modeling, and data analysis** to better understand the processes that produce intense precipitation, thunderstorms, lightning, and other forms of extreme weather.

The **X** in Meso-WX reflects the intersections at the heart of our research: **mesoscale processes × observations × modeling × extremes**.

---

## 🌩️ What we study

Our research spans several connected areas of atmospheric science:

* **Mesoscale meteorology**
* **Severe convective thunderstorms**
* **Extreme precipitation and high-impact weather**
* **Radar meteorology and remote sensing of atmosphere**
* **Lightning and storm electrification**
* **Cloud and precipitation microphysics**
* **Aerosol-cloud–precipitation interactions**
* **Numerical modeling of clouds and storms**

We are especially interested in questions such as:

> What controls the initiation, organization, and intensification of deep convection?

> Why do some storms produce extreme rainfall, frequent lightning, or other hazardous weather?

> How do aerosols, microphysics, dynamics, and environmental conditions interact within convective systems?

> How can radar, lightning observations, satellites, and numerical models be combined to understand storms more completely?

> How do convective processes scale up to influence regional precipitation and weather extremes?

---

## 🛰️ How we approach these questions

Meso-WX sits at the intersection of **observations and modeling**.

### Observations & Remote Sensing

We use measurements from platforms such as:

* Doppler Weather Radars, including polarimetric radar
* Lightning detection networks
* Geostationary and polar-orbiting satellites
* Surface and upper-air observations
* Reanalysis and other atmospheric datasets

A major goal is to extract physically meaningful information from observations rather than treating them simply as datasets.

### Numerical Modeling

We use cloud-resolving and mesoscale numerical weather prediction models to investigate processes that cannot be fully isolated from observations alone.

Our modeling interests include:

* Convection initiation and organization
* Storm dynamics
* Cloud and precipitation microphysics
* Extreme rainfall
* Lightning-related microphysical processes
* Observation–model comparisons

### Data, Computation & Visualization

Atmospheric science increasingly involves working with large and complex datasets. Students in the group can develop experience with:

`Python` · `Linux` · `Git/GitHub` · `xarray` · `NumPy` · `SciPy` · `Matplotlib` · `WRF` · radar and satellite data · high-performance computing

We value **reproducible research, open scientific workflows, and clear visualization of atmospheric processes**.

## 🔬 Research philosophy

At Meso-WX, we are interested in research that connects **physical understanding with quantitative analysis**.

A good research question, in our view, is not simply:

> What happened?

but also:

> **Why did it happen, how can we test that explanation, and does the same mechanism operate in other storms or environments?**

We encourage students to move back and forth between observations, theory, and models, and to think critically about what each can and cannot tell us.

## 🌍 Beyond individual storms

Although mesoscale weather is at the core of Meso-WX, we are interested in the broader consequences of convective processes.

Clouds and storms connect atmospheric scales. Individual convective systems can influence regional precipitation, hydrology, atmospheric circulation, and weather extremes.

Understanding these connections is one of the central challenges of atmospheric science, and one of the reasons the **X** in Meso-WX matters.
